What are ragi sangati and natukodi pulusu?

Ragi sangati is a cooked finger-millet preparation shaped into portions and eaten with a gravy. Ragi means finger millet. In TSK's combination, the sangati contains both finger millet and rice.

The Ananthapuramu district guide identifies ragi sangati, also known as ragi mudda, with Rayalaseema food. Recipes vary, so a regional description does not establish every restaurant's ingredients.

Natukodi pulusu is country chicken in a sour gravy, commonly made with tamarind. You may also see the name written as naatu kodi or naatukodi. Telugu recipe writer Vismai Food describes this curry and its pairing with ragi sangati.

The two parts are eaten together: sangati provides a soft base, and pulusu adds gravy and chicken. For other regional dishes on our menu, explore TSK's signature plates from Andhra Pradesh and Telangana.

Should you order plain sangati or the chicken combination?

Choose the combination when you want sangati with country chicken curry in the same order. Choose plain sangati when you want to select an accompaniment separately. Our menu lists these as two distinct dishes.

How do you eat ragi sangati with natukodi pulusu?

Take a small piece of sangati, combine it with some pulusu and eat the chicken alongside it. A spoon works well if you prefer one.

  1. Allow the food to cool to a comfortable temperature.
  2. Take a small piece of sangati with your fingers or a spoon.
  3. Dip it into the pulusu or add a little gravy to your spoon.
  4. Eat the two together, then add chicken and more gravy as you prefer.

Our menu describes the curry as fiery. Start with a little gravy to judge the chilli level, and tell the team about your spice preference before ordering. Any adjustment depends on the available preparation options.
